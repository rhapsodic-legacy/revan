## 1. The setting

### The galaxy and the Galactic Unity
The **Galactic Unity** governs the galaxy's core worlds. It is old, **autocratic, corrupt, and drowning in bureaucracy**, and it grew fat because it believed darkness was gone. Many **Unity Guardsmen** are little more than rent-a-goons in nice armour. **Unity forces speak in an Elizabethan register** ("yonder," "prithee"), a quiet way to show how old the institution is.

Every world keeps its own defence fleet for its own skies. When the Srov ask worlds to lend battleships to hunt the rogue Vors fleets, most refuse: giving up ships weakens their own defence, and rogue "Armada" ships haven't attacked *them*.

### Eternity and Seers
**Eternity** is the mystic force. Those who wield it are **Seers**, and each Seer's way with it is mystical and unique. Seers carry **ornate swords** (eastern and western inspired, wildly elaborate, each reflecting its wielder), which they ignite telepathically in a crackling field of energy that cuts any material. Seer orders are also warrior orders, like samurai or templars.

### The orders and peoples (and their sound)
| Faction | Who they are | Sound | Look |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Srov** | The order of light Seers. They hunt the scattered Vors and demand total loyalty from their own. They enforce the belief that the Immortal Empire is long dead, and many Srov believe it. | Wild, mystical, vocal-heavy desert ritual | Temples, robes, lacquered curved swords |
| **The Vors** | An ancient warrior-mage order of dark Seers. "Vors" is a Grandmaster's title. Wiped out by the Srov when they boarded Akulsa's ship; Akulsa is the last. | Grand full-orchestra march | Gothic, black iron and crimson, cathedral-hilted greatswords |
| **The Immortal Empire** | Legendary and terrifying: they worship dark gods some believe still exist, and perform human sacrifice. Everyone believes they were wiped out long ago. Ruled by **Emperor Voidreaper** from the **Unknown Regions**. | Dirge: dark, heavy | Black, quiet |
| **Everyone else** (the majority) | The Unity's worlds and people | 70s–80s synthwave / neon | Neonpunk: chrome, neon, 80s ships and architecture |

## 2. History (Jesse's canon)

- **The Fall (~2,500 years ago):** civil war stretched across most of the galaxy; many worlds were sundered, and a way of life that spanned the galaxy was lost (some of it still lost). Before the Fall, the galaxy was governed by the **USNC (United Star Nations Council)**; the Galactic Unity replaced it. Countess Viviana comes from a forgotten pre-Fall super-soldier program.
- **Vors Akulsa** knew of the **Immortal Empire** and its Emperor and built an **Armada** to face it. Juggernaut-class cruisers not seen in thousands of years tore through the core worlds, whose defence was people's own flotillas of smuggler craft with stolen laser cannons: **a hundred worlds fell in less than a hundred days.**
- Akulsa took **Tyranus** from the Emperor's worlds as a child, to raise the most powerful apprentice he could against the Emperor. He told Tyranus he was captured from a world where families with children potent in Eternity are taken, the parents sacrificed and the children trained for the Emperor's Immortal Army, and that the Emperor cut down his mother and sacrificed his father to his dark gods. (In a way, true.) **The truth: the Emperor is Tyranus's father.**
- **12 years ago: Vors Kultaka betrayed Akulsa**, an infamous act most people know. The Srov boarded Akulsa's ship. Akulsa was taken prisoner and memory-wiped, and so was his very young apprentice, Tyranus.
- The Srov gave Tyranus **false memories**: village burned and parents killed, sold to slaver mercenaries, freed by the Srov who saw his talent, then a cave trial where something happened (a cave-in) and he was struck in the head by a rock, which is why his early days seem hazy. He woke in the Srov temple medbay at seven, renamed **Dust of the Sun** ("Dust"): an unsettling quiet with so much life and potential within, like the dust around a sun. **All fabricated by the Srov.**
- Akulsa became **Ashe Dawnbringer**, in Srov training. His penchant for direct force put him on **"indefinite sabbatical" five years ago**. Fourteen-year-old Dust snuck aboard Ashe's ship and never left. The Srov stopped asking for Dust back about four years ago and appointed Ashe his Master. Ashe rejects it: *"One must be a master, to be someone's Master."*
- The Vors still have **thousands of ships, including scores of Juggernaut-class battlecruisers**, running amok and mostly hiding. The Srov hunt them.
- **Kultaka has never been found.** Someone is rounding up Akulsa's broken forces. A brilliant **Dark Seer** is manipulating events: Srov are dying, whole flotillas vanish, and Melonsi's party was ambushed through betrayal from within. The Srov think it's Kultaka. **It is Emperor Voidreaper.**
- Kultaka is the Emperor's apprentice. The Emperor promises him immortality, and conquest of this galaxy and the millions beyond.

### The crew before the game
Ashe and Dust as mercenaries. Dust "won" the ***Galactic Companion*** (repainted *Galactic C~~ompanion~~hampion*, "the *Galactic C*") in a con on a fixed race, but the gangers had let him win to frame him; Ashe and Dust can never return to that planet (13 counts of murder). Ashe bought **Deena Ateray** out of slavery to a crime lord, and he and Dust "encouraged" the crime lord to leave her alone. **K'saabu** appeared on the ship one day and stayed.

## 4. ACT I: The Rescue
**Jesse's plot:** the Srov send Ashe and Dust (with Deena and K'saabu) to rescue **Princess Melonsi Ananna**, a powerful Srov Seer who was on a core world asking it to join the Srov hunt for the scattered Vors. Her party was betrayed from within and ambushed. All were thought dead, but her body was never found, so she's thought captured. Ashe believes the mission is a covert way to get him and Dust back into Srov training. The crew crash-lands in the city's slums (the Opening) and fights through the **urban neonpunk jungle** to rescue her.

### 4.1 The gang (Jesse's, 2026-11-03)
When the crew crash-lands (the Opening), **gangers start yelling at them for crashing into their warehouse.** [Gang name: OPEN]
- **Who they are:** a big gang, well connected, and a bunch of idiots (Jesse's touchstone: the punk gang in *Crocodile Dundee II*). Dangerous enough that Ashe's crew won't engage them lightly; idiotic enough that talking your way to a solution is easy enough.
- **Morally ambiguous**, especially on a first playthrough. They are definitely a gang: they beat people up, are at war with other gangs (and kill other gangers), rob, vandalise, and even take slaves. But any slave who works for them for a year becomes a ganger, and they won't enslave children (**Ganger's Honour**). There is something endearing about these violent idiots: the dumpster-fire trash that even the dumpster rejected. They aren't prejudiced against any alien species and only care about **loyalty and competence.**
- **The design point:** the player gets a very familiar circumstance (a home base, quests, humble beginnings, different ways to solve a mission), and will likely grow fond of the gang, or at least some of its members. It's the player's **first experience of long-term benefits from choices made**; the gang's turf becomes a home base of sorts.

**The first confrontation (a dialogue tutorial).**
- The player gets dialogue options, some **greyed out by stats and niche awarenesses.** Tutorial prompts explain that an outcome might not be immediately obvious, and that different outcomes are possible based on choices.
- **Talk your way out (best):** an award with a humorous name ("Peaceful Idiots" or similar) and a dollop of XP, **more than fighting gives.**
- **Fight (least optimal):** the least XP, the least gear, and no hookups (the gang opens up options for the area). If the player starts the fight, a prompt tells them **they can't win this fight, but can survive it by escaping the facility.**
- **At war with the gang:** the player loses everything the gang offers, and the area becomes much harder. Fun for veterans, and it can come with an award (e.g. if certain members are dead by the end of the chapter).

**Side content:** the gang leads to side quests, and perhaps an optional crewmate, **Taz the Spaz**, who wields an electro-axe and uses combat drugs.

**The deal (likely outcome of meeting their leader).**
- A couple of the other gangs are getting gear from **"Upside"** (the area beyond the slums; name tentative). It's weird that anyone would trade with gangers, and gangers don't have many Galactic Credits, so these non-retrofitted weapons aren't being bought outright. Something's not quite right. The gang doesn't really care; other gangs getting better weapons is bad for them.
- If Ashe and his crew put a stop to it (wipe those gangs out, and maybe find the benefactor and "convince" them to deal with this gang, and only this gang), the gang will tell Ashe where to find the right parts to fix his ship. **In the meantime, they hold the *Galactic C* as collateral.**

**Pointing the crew to Melonsi.** The gang says they kept their end of the bargain. They don't know anything about a princess in the slums ("if there was a princess who looked like that, we'd know"). But there are a couple of **cartels** the crew should introduce themselves to: brokers of information, far better placed to know Upside business or how to get it. **One cartel is known for trading secrets in return for things.** That's the first place, and it's where **Melonsi is hiding as the apothecary.**

**Ashe's warning (earlier in the act).** Developers' excavations surface an **impossibly ancient vault**, from before **the Fall**. Ashe warns the gang that the plunder might be promising, but who knows what could be in such a thing. They go anyway.

### 4.2 Return to the warehouse: the massacre (Jesse's)
After rescuing Melonsi, the crew returns for the ship, and **everyone is dead.**
- A creepy fog has settled over the area. Lights blink or are out; doors swing on their hinges. **Abominations** roam (six-legged dogs with glowing red eyes, and other shadow things), and the party must defeat them.
- **Search reveals survivors** holed up in an old meat freezer: children and young mothers. Outside it, bodies hang upside down, drained of blood. (Shot: their shadowy silhouettes against a fire-lit wall, one bloody hand hanging in view, so the player deduces what they're seeing.)
- The survivors can only speak of screaming, which stopped about 24 hours ago. One hands Ashe a **journal** while Melonsi heals the wounded.

**The journal** (optional reading the player can keep; it opens dialogue options). It reads much like the ship captain's log in *Dracula*.
- The gangers brought back many strange artefacts from the vault, including a **6-foot metal box**, clearly hollow (something inside bangs about when shaken), with no seam or lid, protected by an energy field that only a password on a keypad can disable.
- Innocent tidings at first: gangers going missing, always ones alone and away from the others. Nothing odd there; all sorts of nasties roam the slums and pick off idiots who walk alone in the shadows.
- But the disappearances were constant. People vanished from rooms where they were alone. People spoke of a tall, thin woman with ash-white skin and deadly beauty, who moved like a ghost and was thought to be one.
- Gangers on high alert, safeties off, were taken without a shot fired. Someone looking through the treasure room saw the metal box **open**. Inside was a crimson sheet, like the bed a dead person would lie in.
- Pages are unreadable from blood. It skips forward: *"They figured out... the she-ghost came in that metal box. They tried to kill it, and became its victims. Almost everyone is dead now. She hangs them upside down to get all their blood... Nosferatu... Nosferatu... That's what she is..."*

### 4.3 Viviana aboard the *Galactic C* (Jesse's)
- On the ship, **Melonsi senses Viviana right away.** They corner her, ready to kill her.
- **Viviana's account:** she killed many gangers, the ones who tried to kill her. Everyone else was killed by the shadow monstrosities. She hangs the dead upside down not to feed, but because it's part of her **mental programming**: it causes fear in the enemies of the **USNC** (United Star Nations Council, the galactic federation before the Fall, which the Galactic Unity replaced). She can't help her programming; resisting it causes her severe mental pain. She didn't kill anyone who didn't try to kill her, but she did kill many gangers. (Images flash of the gangers she killed, alive as the player knew them.)
- **Tone:** morally ambiguous and melancholic.
- **Melonsi still wants to kill her,** and is dissuaded:
  1. First, Viviana says the only reason the darkness creatures didn't get the people in the meat freezer is that she hung all those rotting bodies around it, **masking the scent.**
  2. Second, Viviana asks Melonsi where she got an item she's wearing: very high fashion, something Viviana hasn't seen since before she was taken for experimentation. Bizarrely, this earns her more mercy than anything else she says. (Funny in the moment, and telling of how far Melonsi is from not being ego-driven.)
- The dialogue here has lots of role-play opportunity; to be worked out together.

Once aboard the repaired *Galactic C*, **Melonsi uses her power to force the crew to the Srov homeworld.**

## 5. ACT II: The Trials (the Srov homeworld)
**Jesse's plot:**
- The Srov rule: Ashe and Dust must **finish their Srov training or be killed**. They "know enough about Eternity to become future Vors acolytes, but not enough to stop that from happening," so they'd be hunted for their power.
- **The trials** span many paths across the planet [to be designed]. They culminate in **Ashe and Dust being forced to fight each other.**
- The Srov demand loyalty to them first. Ashe is furious and attacks the High Council and Melonsi, and is easily batted down by their powers. *"Either one of you kills the other, or you both die."*
- **The fight:** the player, as Dust, cannot match Ashe however carefully they play. Ashe is about to kill himself to save Dust; he can't bring himself to kill his apprentice and friend.
- **Dust outwits the Council** (§5.1).
- **Vasili** declares the test passed and tells Melonsi she'll join them. She storms off, knowing she has no choice. (Vasili believes she is giving in to her princess entitlement to a dangerous degree, and wants her to remember who she is.)
- **Maul Acido** gives them the mission (§5.2).

### 5.1 The loophole (Jesse's scene)
> **DUST:** "May I ask a question, before my death?"
> *(They grant it.)*
> **DUST:** "Are Ashe and I Srov now?"
> *(The Council laughs.)* "No."
> **DUST:** "Then why are we being made to prove total loyalty to the Srov? We're just civilians with swords."
> **VASILI** *(speaking for the first time; until now he has said nothing, and never demanded that either of them kill the other)*: "Srov in training, you are. Not civilians."
> **DUST:** "So does that make us Srov, then? You said Srov must be totally loyal to Srov. Now you're inventing a new category—"
> **MELONSI** *(cutting him off)*: "Yes. You are Srov. No entitled duties, no riches, nothing, but Srov. Srov in training. Ashe, kill him."
> **DUST** *(raising his hands)*: "Then his loyalty to me is loyalty to the Srov, by your own reasoning. And I say: 'Ashe, don't kill me.'"
> *(The Council tries to argue.)*
> **DUST:** "Which is it? Either we're civilians and we don't have to prove our loyalty, or we're Srov and I'm telling Ashe not to kill me. Besides, isn't there some supreme law about Srov not killing each other?"
> *(Vasili is laughing.)*
> **ASHE** *(helping Dust up)*: "Good goin', kid."
> **MELONSI** *(enraged at being upstaged by learners, drawing her sword)*: "Fine. Then I'll take you both."
> **VASILI** *(stopping her)*: "No. The test is passed. Not by sword, by blood, or by allegiance. But by a mouth, a mind, and two hearts." *(beat)* "...Now three hearts."
> *(Vasili tells Melonsi she will be joining them. She storms off.)*

### 5.2 The mission
> **ASHE:** "What, dare I ask, is this adventure?"
> **MAUL ACIDO:** "Vors Kultaka has never been found. Someone out there is rounding up Akulsa's broken forces. If our fears are true, a second invasion won't be far away. And... there's more."

*"There's more":* a brilliant Dark Seer seems to be manipulating events. Srov are dying, and whole flotillas of ships vanish; that's how Melonsi was ambushed. The Srov believe it's Kultaka. It's Emperor Voidreaper.

## 6. ACT III: World to World
**Jesse's plot:** going from world to world, picking up more crewmates. **A time of innocence and fun.**
- **Why it's long and light (Jesse):** this act is massive character building, and it's what makes everything after it land. If the game is gripping and tense from the first moment, it burns a person out. There *are* tense moments, but they're about the characters, not the central plot. The relationships built here are what make Act V so hard: people the player has come to love die.
- **Crewmates:** many are recruitable, and the player can only take some. A few tie directly into the story; the rest tie into specific worlds, so that Act V's tragedies hit harder. Candidates: `08_CREW_CANDIDATES.md` [proposed].

## 7. ACT IV: Noticed
**Jesse's plot:** the plot thickens. The crew's heroism and mischief haven't gone unnoticed. Romance options intensify.

## 8. ACT V: The Second Invasion
**Jesse's plot:** things take a hard turn. **The second invasion begins.** The player must choose **who lives and who dies**, which can make crewmates leave and romances end or intensify.

## 9. ACT VI: The Grand Ball
**Jesse's plot:**
- **The first big reveal: Ashe Dawnbringer is Vors Akulsa.** Kultaka names him, and speaks of serving a master who promises immortality and conquest of this galaxy and the millions beyond. Ashe knows who the Emperor is, and that this is why he built his Armada. He gives no specifics: only that the Emperor is terrifyingly strong in Eternity, as strong as Akulsa, possibly stronger.
- **The grand ball** happens in this act. Deena and the others get to dress up. **The ball is ambushed**, and that's how people get captured.
- **Kultaka is defeated too easily, and "flees."**

## 10. ACT VII: The Snake's Head
**Jesse's plot:**
- The heroes regroup. Kultaka fled to the Unknown Regions **without removing the tracking beacon**, so it's plainly a trap. But the invasion continues, and until the head of the snake is cut off, billions will die.
- **Romances play out**, bittersweet, in neonpunk / synthwave style. Ashe and his romance, and Dust and his, go back and forth; each pairing's contrast illustrates the other.
- In the Unknown Regions they are, predictably, **ambushed and brought to a space station built of concentric rings**, like a ringed planet (original design).
- **The final reveal: the Emperor is Dust's father.** It eases a fear Dust has carried:
  > **DUST:** "Ashe knows he was the last of the Vors, the dark Seers. That explains why he sometimes does dark things. But what about me? I was a kid. I couldn't even swing a sword. And that same terrible darkness comes off me. Am I just... wrong?"
  Learning he is Emperor Voidreaper's son puts that fear to rest.
- Reveal scene (Jesse's structure and lines; the reveal itself is worded our own way, not as the film's line):
  > **EMPEROR:** "Akulsa never told you where you came from."
  > **DUST:** "I was taken from your worlds when Akulsa attacked you."
  > **EMPEROR:** *(a deep bass, tinny laugh)* "Yes, that is true. But you are so strong with Eternity... is it mere chance that he found you? Where did you come from? Who were your parents?"
  > **DUST** *(defiant)*: "He told me you cut down my mother, and sacrificed my father to your dark gods."
  > **EMPEROR:** "No, Tyranus. **You are my son.**"
  > *(Akulsa admits it: he took Tyranus to make the most powerful apprentice to fight the Emperor. Kultaka and the Emperor gloat.)*
  > **EMPEROR** *(offering his hand)*: "Join me, son. Together we will become the dark gods, immortal and omnipotent."
  > *(Kultaka gloats over Akulsa: this is what comes to those who follow the Emperor.)*
- **Final battle (Jesse's):** Akulsa and Tyranus vs Kultaka and the Emperor. **Kultaka must go down first.** If the players focus the Emperor instead, he **life-drains Kultaka** to keep fighting, which eventually drops Kultaka to 0 HP anyway.
- **After the Emperor falls (Jesse's scene):**
  - Ashe begins reciting the **Rubricon of the Dark Seers**, the Vors' own creed about what makes a Seer. To the Emperor: *"You are powerful, but you are no Vors. Your way is one of ego, which leads to self-destruction. Vors are not afraid of the dark, and know that in the silence there are answers."* (Wording to be worked out with Jesse.) Ashe is saying all of this **for Kultaka's sake.**
  - Ashe drives his sword through the Emperor. The Emperor's raspy bass laugh doesn't stop, and he says nothing. Ashe beheads him, and **the laughter keeps coming from the air itself.**
  - Dust uses Eternity to seize the body and head, throws them into an airlock, jettisons them, and hurls them at great speed toward the **red giant** the station orbits.
  - Ashe **heals Kultaka**: the galaxy needs to know the way of the dark as much as the way of the light. Kultaka swears revenge. Ashe: *"As is only proper for the last of the Vors, the Supreme Being of the Dark Seers."*
  - All the while, Dust stands at the port window facing the sun. Eventually, as the voice-overs play: *"That dark god that was once my father is trying to regain control of his body. I'm making sure it enters the sun, where even he will die."*

## 11. EPILOGUE
**Jesse's plot:** all the player's choices play out, and the main story finishes, with one last twist:
- **Ashe anoints Kultaka as the Supreme Being of the Vors**, the last of their order. **Akulsa is declared gone.**
- **Dust is made Emperor of the Immortal Empire** (his birthright).

**Ashe's voice-over** explains why:
- *The galaxy grew corpulent and corrupt when it thought darkness was gone. Darkness can grow in any person; it doesn't need to wield a sword. Kultaka, deprived of my Armada, is a threat that will unite the failing Galactic Unity, but a predictable and manageable one.* (Kultaka is portrayed as a brutish villain, not a cunning one.)
- *The Immortal Empire, if it ever realised it, could conquer this unprepared galaxy in a hundred days. That's why I built my Armada: to face them. Dust will make sure they never learn of it, and one day the two peoples can unite. Also, he's still green behind the ears. The Srov would keep him on a very short leash once they found out his birthright. Living in the Unknown Regions, he'll be left alone.*
