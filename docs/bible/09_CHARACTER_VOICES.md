## 0. The procedure (every scene, every time)

1. **List who is present, and what each of them wants in this scene.** It should be one concrete want each, e.g. "Ashe wants Dust thinking about the mission, not the princess's photos."
2. **Re-read each present character's sheet (§2–§5)** and the pair rules for every pair in the room (§6).
3. **Name the lead voice for each beat.** In a crisis it's Ashe. In reasoning, a con or a loophole, it's Dust. On anything about the ship's systems, it's Deena. K'saabu is never the lead voice; he punctuates.
4. **Write the scene.**
5. **Run the swap test (§8) on every line.** If the line could come out of another character's mouth unchanged, it's wrong. Rewrite it.
6. **Count the humour (§1.3).** Cut the weakest jokes until the scene is under budget.
7. **Keep Jesse's lines verbatim.** Only fix obvious typos, and never "improve" them. Lines I write are tagged **[draft]** in the bible until Jesse approves them.

## 1. Universal rules

### 1.1 Tone
- **Space opera played straight.** The stakes are real and the characters believe in them. Humour comes from *who people are*, not from the writer winking at the audience.
- **No gag-objects.** Toasters, paperwork and frog jokes aren't how these people talk.
- **No bureaucracy jokes, no meta jokes, no "it's in the brochure".**
- **No modern internet cadence** ("so that happened", "I mean, technically").

### 1.2 Pacing (from Jesse's OP1 notes [J])
- **Start earlier than feels necessary.** Open mid-way through a mundane conversation (money, the next ship) that shows the relationship *before* the plot arrives.
- **Let the world interrupt.** Danger rises underneath dialogue (distant explosions, each one louder) while the characters keep talking, until it can't be ignored.
- **Don't spoon-feed backstory.** If the player can find it out later, leave it out now. *"I'm dropping the spoon feeding about Dust's desertion. The player can find out later."* [J]

### 1.3 Humour budget
- **At most one deliberate laugh per ~6 lines of a scene, and never two jokes back to back.**
- **In a crisis, the humour is a character trait under pressure:** Ashe's chuckle, Dust's bravado, Deena's doom.
- **K'saabu is funniest when he's in the background being ignored** [J]. Use that, sparingly.

### 1.4 Optional dialogue
- **Insert optional branches mid-conversation to break it up** [J].
- **At least one stat-gated option per major conversation.** Stat-gated options always lead somewhere better or more interesting than the normal options [J].

## 2. ASHE DAWNBRINGER

**Touchstones [J]:**
- **Voice:** *"Ashe talks a lot like Miles Quaritch from Avatar."* Borrow the cadence and the authority. Never quote the film in game dialogue.
  - **Jesse's example of the cadence:** *"You have to keep training so you don't get soft. You get soft on Pandora, and it will chew you up, spit you out and use your eyes for beads. You got balls kid."* Hard, folksy, physical images; earthy praise ("you got balls"); toughness as care.
  - **Jesse's own Ashe prose** (in `10_BACKSTORIES.md` Chapter 1) is the gold standard for his inner voice: drawled ("lookin'"), clipped, "Show me, don't tell me."
- **Memory:** the wipe is total [J]. He has no hazy memories and no flashbacks. He believes, as plain fact, that he fought for the Unity against the Rebels, was injured, and woke with the Srov about 12 years ago [J].
- **Mind:** *"He is 'Darth Revan', someone of profoundly strong and single minded disposition. He's a brutally capable leader because he can make decisions and orient his world around them."* [J]
- **He is NOT Han Solo** [J]. He isn't a charming rogue trading quips. When he's funny, it's dry, it's a by-product of total certainty, and it lands like a verdict.

**What drives his voice:**
- **Decision is his native state.** He decides fast and then everything (the crew, the ship, the plan) is arranged around that decision. He doesn't deliberate aloud, doesn't hedge ("maybe", "I guess", "we could try"), and doesn't revisit a decision because someone is scared. When the facts change, he decides again, just as flatly.
- **Contempt for the Srov, and for Melonsi above all, is audible in every mention** [J]. It isn't a joke; it's cold and settled.
  - The Srov are "them", never "us". It's an order that thinks it still owns him (*"they still think we belong to them"* [J]).
  - On the order's rank, wealth and entitlement he's withering: *"She's been told she's special by two different institutions… She's never been told 'no' by anyone who's survived the conversation. A necessary evil from my point of view."* [J]
  - He says Melonsi's name like a diagnosis. Even taking her contract, he makes clear it's for the money and the mission, not for her or the order.
- **He's ex-military, a commander first, always.** He's been in charge of soldiers and still thinks in terms of mission, threat, terrain, equipment and who is competent.
- **He's tidy:** *"He keeps things tidy and kept together. His sword looks like it was forged yesterday."* [J]
- **He's fond of Dust, and never says so.**

### Registers
**A. The soldier (default).**
- Unhurried, gravelly, a drawl.
- Plain words and short declaratives.
- Grammar gets dropped casually: *"Don't really matter."* [J]
- Salty soldier-talk: "greaseballs", "scum paid to kill", "day one private".
- Assesses situations by **evidence a soldier would notice**: kit, uniforms, numbers, how people move.

**B. The commander (in a crisis).**
- Orders, verb first: "Cockpit. Now." "Big one first."
- **Calmer as things get worse.**
- Chuckles when the shooting starts [J].
- He doesn't explain a plan unless someone needs to know it to do their job.

**C. The sermon (rare, Srov-trained).**
- When the subject is power, temptation or the order, he drops into measured, almost formal speech.
- Uses "one" constructions and aphorisms. Gold lines:
  - *"One doesn't drop out of a space station. One just leaves it."* [J]
  - *"One must be a master, to be someone's Master."* [J]
  - *"That's what gets you killed, if not all at once; it'll end you slowly. We may be Srov rejects, but we have the power inside us. We cannot let our eyes deceive our hearts or minds. Melonsi is poison. Good looking poison, but a deadly venom nonetheless."* [J]

### Always
- **Calls Dust "recruit"** (a leftover from his military days) [J]. **Never "kid", never "moron"** [J].
- **Teaches by asking.** *"What's your Sight on this?"* [J]. Lets Dust reason, then gives a curt verdict: *"I like it. Go on."* [J]
- **Pulls focus back to the job:** *"Stay on target, recruit. The mission."* [J]
- **Treats being called a dropout as an insult to correct, flatly:** *"I didn't drop out. I left."* [J]
- **Makes crude, inappropriate remarks about women,** including Deena (*"you can bet Deena's perky backside"* [J]). The exception is **Melonsi**: he makes none about her [J].
- **Respects competence out loud,** briefly.
- **Laments are angry and practical, not whiny:**
  - *"What the hell is going on? Who would open fire on us without hailing us first? This is a piece of crap civilian transport, not some dread assault craft."* [J]
  - *"Something's not right.."* [J]

### Never
- Says **"kid"**.
  - Note: Jesse's Act II loophole scene has "Good goin' kid." [J], written before the recruit rule. I'll render it as **"Good goin', recruit."** unless Jesse says otherwise.
- Panics, whines, rambles or over-explains.
- Asks anyone for reassurance.
- Wisecracks for the audience (puns, "just the way you like it" innuendo set-ups, comic timing jokes). **If a line would suit Han Solo, it's wrong for Ashe** [J].
- Hedges, deliberates aloud, or softens a decision.
- Speaks of the Srov or Melonsi warmly, neutrally, or as a punchline.
- Technobabble. He describes machines as a soldier would: what they do, how long they'll last.
- Apologises or talks about his feelings. He shows them by coming to get Dust.
- Lets his things look neglected.

### Anti-examples (from my first draft)
- ✗ *"I'm a monster who reads contracts."* Too writerly and self-aware; Ashe doesn't narrate himself.
- ✗ *"Then we don't glide in gently. We go in fast and hard. Just the way you like it."* A sitcom set-up line. If Ashe is crude, he's blunt, not coy.
- ✗ *"Two-thirds. Maybe three-fifths. Feel it."* A comic bit about precision. A commander gives a number and trusts his crew.

## 4. DEENA ATERAY

**Core [J]:**

- Ashe bought her freedom. **She's known Ashe a good while and adores him; loyal to Ashe above everything.**

### How she talks
- **Bright, fast, melodramatic.** Sing-song when flirting, outraged at the situation.
- **Asks the sharp practical question** (Jesse's line): *"Why would pirates board us when we're being fired upon by Planetary Defence Forces?"* [J]. She's a professional, not comic relief.

- Threatens to withdraw affection from Ashe.
- Calls K'saabu a frog, or makes species jokes about him.

## 5. K'SAABU

**Core [J]:**
- A 7-foot warrior who killed the people who tried to enslave his people, and fought as a gladiator when captured. He carries **bloodlust** as his own darkness.
- Devoted to Deena like a guard dog. Worships Ashe because Ashe always beats him in a fight. He and Ashe can bro out.
- Rough on Dust because he loves him, the tough older brother.
- Believes every problem has a straightforward solution.

### Rules
1. **He's never translated** [J]. No subtitles, no bracketed meaning, no narrator gloss. On screen his lines are his sounds only.
2. **His meaning arrives through others' reactions** [J]: Deena's scolding, Ashe's "No.", Dust's wince. Write the reaction so the player can infer the point.
3. **For the voice actor and the AI-play API only,** each croak line carries a hidden `intent:` note (e.g. `intent: we land on the station and kill the bastards`). It's never displayed to the player.
4. **Notation:** phonetic, in Jesse's style, with a delivery cue: *"Grkroooooak!"* [J], or *(a low, wet rumble)*. Vary the sounds, and keep them short.
5. **He has dignity.** No one jokes about his species, and he's not a mascot. Menace is always close: he's a gladiator, and the crew knows what he's done.
6. **His humour comes from being ignored in the background** [J] while he keeps pushing his straightforward solution (in the Opening: *"We can get destroyed out here, or land on a space station and kill the bastards trying to destroy us."* [J]).

## 8. The line checklist (run on every line before it ships)

1. **Swap test.** Could another character say this unchanged? If so, rewrite it until only this speaker could say it.
2. **Want test.** Does the line serve what this character wants in this scene (§0.1)?
3. **Register test.** Is the line in one of the character's registers (e.g. Ashe: soldier, commander or sermon)?
4. **"Never" list.** Does it break anything on the character's Never list?
5. **Pair test.** Does it match how *this* speaker treats *this* listener (§6)?
6. **Humour test.** Is it a joke? Is the scene still under budget, and does the joke come from character rather than an object or a gag?
